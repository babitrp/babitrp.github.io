01 The problem

From scattered listings to one audited review.

Safety reviewers needed a patient's labs, adverse events, medications, exposure and history side by side. Each listing came from a separate programming request, reviews were assembled by hand, and there was no shared record of who reviewed what, against which version of the data.

02 Why existing approaches fell short

What the old workflow couldn't do.

Listings and static patient profiles are produced per request and can't be filtered, linked or annotated. General BI tools don't understand ADaM structures, and none of them keeps a validated record of who reviewed what, against which data.

What I personally designed, built and led.

My role

Lead developer of a small team. I own the application end to end: requirements with client stakeholders, architecture, delivery, validation and releases.

  • The append-only audit store, with salted and hashed subject identifiers and data-version signatures on every review.

05 Difficult engineering decisions

Decisions, trade-offs and how quality was assured.

Decisions & trade-offs
  • Append-only audit tables. Nothing is updated or deleted, so the record cannot be rewritten. Trade-off: more rows to store, and every read has to resolve the latest state.
  • Session-scoped state over globals. Concurrent reviewers can never see each other's data. Trade-off: more plumbing, because modules receive state as arguments instead of reaching for it.
  • Background workers for heavy work. Long derivations no longer freeze the interface. Trade-off: workers don't share the app's environment, so every dependency is passed in explicitly.
  • One sourced application, not an R package. Simpler to deploy and validate as a single unit. Trade-off: no namespace boundary, so naming discipline and tests carry more weight.
Challenges
  • Many reviewers, one server. All per-user state is session-scoped, so concurrent sessions never see each other's data.
  • A record that holds up to audit. Audit tables are append-only, subject identifiers are salted and hashed before storage, and each review is bound to a data-version signature, so it lapses if the data changes.
  • Heavy views that stay responsive. Long computations run in background workers and plots are cached on safe keys.
  • Change control in a validated system. Change-request references on every PR, dual approval to deploy, automated security scanning and unit, integration and end-to-end tests with coverage checks.
Validation

Formally GxP-validated. I wrote the validation evidence: test reports and traceability from requirements to tests.

What it does

Study views to find a signal, patient views to explain it.

01 Load

Data upload

Reads CSV, SAS, XPT, JSON and Parquet. One click loads a sample study with labs, vitals, adverse events, medications and exposure, ready to review without a programming request.

02 Study

Clinical Insights

Opens on a study summary: how many patients and how they split. Population, arm and demographic filters apply to every section.

Adverse events by organ class and term, worst severity and time to first event. Signal detection compares terms between any active arm and a chosen reference arm. Organ safety covers liver, kidney, blood and heart. Labs show maximum post-baseline toxicity grade and PCSA level, and mean change from baseline, as do vitals and ECG.

03 Drill down

Every number is a way in

Click the deaths tile to list the patients behind it, then filter the patient view to just them. From a study signal to the people behind it in two clicks.

04 Ask

Smart Filters

Plain-language questions, such as patients with ALT above three times the upper limit, become an exact filter. The language model sees only the schema and metadata, never patient values, and each generated filter is validated before it runs.

05 Patient

One timeline per patient

Labs, adverse events, medications and exposure on one axis, with medical history overlaid, so cause and timing are easy to judge. PCSA and toxicity flags use configurable rules. Further tabs cover trends, hepatic, renal and hematology safety, including the eDISH liver plot.

06 Sign off

Review and comment

The reviewer marks the patient reviewed and records a comment. Earlier comments stay on record with who wrote them and when.

Built for a regulated setting

The record matters as much as the result.

The Session Info panel shows what happened, who did it and when.

Audit trail

Data loaded, reviews and comments, each with user and timestamp. Storage is append-only, and subject identifiers are salted and hashed before they are stored.

Integrity

A check confirms the audit chain is unbroken.

Data version

Each review is tied to the data version. If the data changes, the review lapses.

Evidence

Changelog, test results and a package risk assessment show what changed, how the app was tested and how its R packages were assessed.
